# fdr-multiple-testing-correction

## Summary

A screening skill that controls the false discovery rate across the many paired significance tests generated by an in silico perturbation screen, combining Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction with an effect-size cutoff (log2FC > 0.5) to retain credible fate-shifting transcription factor hits.

## When to use

Use when many TF perturbation simulations each yield a p-value and you must correct across all tested targets before calling significant hits (e.g., FDR < 0.01 plus log2FC > 0.5 in a 200+ TF screen).

## When NOT to use

- You have only a single or few hypotheses — plain uncorrected p-values or a Bonferroni-style threshold are simpler and BH adds no benefit
- Perturbation simulations were run once (no random initializations), since paired t-tests across 10 initializations are the basis of the reported p-values
- The question is predictive accuracy of the PRESCIENT model itself (e.g., fate bias prediction on Weinreb et al. lineage tracing), not calling significant hits from a perturbation screen

## Inputs

- Per-TF paired t-test p-values from 10 random-initialization PRESCIENT perturbation simulations (z = 5 overexpression)
- Per-TF log2 fold-change values of fate probabilities (perturbed vs. unperturbed) for alpha-, beta-, and EC-cell fates
- data_pt torch object (normalized expression matrix, gene features, cell type labels, timepoints, PCA/UMAP objects) used to define the TF target list

## Outputs

- Ranked hit table tf_perturbation_hits.csv with columns TF, fate, log2FC, t-statistic, FDR
- Per-fate significant hit lists (e.g., 10 alpha-cell, 18 beta-cell TF hits) at FDR < 0.01 and log2(FC) > 0.5

## How to apply

First compute, for every screened transcription factor, a paired t-test comparing fate probabilities (alpha-, beta-, EC-cell outcomes) between perturbed (z = 5 overexpression) and unperturbed PRESCIENT simulations across 10 random initializations, so the test accounts for simulation stochasticity rather than treating seeds as independent samples. Then apply Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction across the full list of screened TFs for each fate separately, since the family of hypotheses is per-cell-fate. Retain hits passing FDR < 0.01 AND log2(FC) > 0.5 so only both statistically significant and practically large fate shifts survive. Finally write the ranked table (TF, fate, log2FC, t-statistic, FDR) to tf_perturbation_hits.csv so the correction step is auditable and the hit count per fate (e.g., 10 alpha, 18 beta) can be checked against expectations.

## Related tools

- **PRESCIENT** (Generates the perturbed vs. unperturbed fate probability distributions whose paired t-test p-values and log2FC values feed into the Benjamini-Hochberg correction) — https://github.com/gifford-lab/prescient
- **PyTorch** (Holds the data_pt torch object whose gene features and cell type labels define the 200+ TF screening universe across which FDR is corrected)
- **scikit-learn** (Provides the PCA object in data_pt fit to normalized expression that contextualizes the simulated trajectories underlying the per-TF tests)

## Examples

```
prescient process_data --input veres.h5ad  # then, per fate: benjamini_hochberg(paired_ttest_pvals_across_10_inits) and filter FDR < 0.01 & log2FC > 0.5
```

## Evaluation signals

- Every retained hit has BH-corrected FDR < 0.01 AND log2(FC) > 0.5 — no hit passes only one criterion
- Hit counts per fate are reproducible: the screen reported 10 TFs increasing alpha-cell fate and 18 TFs increasing beta-cell fate
- tf_perturbation_hits.csv contains exactly the columns TF, fate, log2FC, t-statistic, FDR, ranked per fate
- p-values are paired t-tests across 10 random initializations, not one-off tests, so re-running with different seeds should not drastically change the FDR ranking
- Corrected FDR values are monotonically consistent with raw p-values (BH property: FDR >= raw p-value for every TF)

## Limitations

- Benjamini-Hochberg control assumes valid, appropriately dependent p-values; PRESCIENT simulations depend on assumptions such as the final time point being at steady-state and growth-rate estimates, so poorly estimated proliferation can distort the tests
- A significant FDR-corrected hit does not imply causality — 'the association of certain genes with specific cell fates does not necessarily imply causality' — and 'there also remain challenges to confidently suggest gene sets for experimental perturbation'
- Perturbations were restricted to transcription factors (non-TF targets like signaling pathway effectors untested), and PCA-space simulation loses information about individual genes; lowly-expressed fate-relevant genes may be dropped altogether
