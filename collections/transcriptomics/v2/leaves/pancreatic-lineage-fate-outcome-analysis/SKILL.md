# pancreatic-lineage-fate-outcome-analysis

## Summary

Uses PRESCIENT in silico TF perturbation simulations on the Veres et al. 2019 in vitro β-cell differentiation time series to quantify how perturbation timing (day 0–6) and starting subpopulation (SOX2+, NKX6.1+, neurog3-early/mid/late) shift β-, α-, and EC-cell fate outcomes.

## When to use

Use when simulating transcription factor overexpression in single-cell time-series scRNA-seq of pancreatic/β-cell differentiation, and you need day-resolved or starting-population-resolved fate-bias shifts toward β-, α-, or EC-cell outcomes.

## When NOT to use

- Your data's final time point is not at steady-state — PRESCIENT requires the last time point to be at steady-state, so temporal perturbation simulations would be invalid
- You need gene-level resolution of perturbation effects rather than fate-outcome shifts, since PRESCIENT transforms data into PCA space where information about individual genes is lost
- The perturbation targets are non-TFs such as signaling pathway effectors — the published screen was limited to TFs, so positive controls and expected-effect priors exist only for TFs

## Inputs

- PRESCIENT data_pt torch file (serialized dict: data, celltype, genes, tps, x, xp, pca, y) generated by `prescient process_data`
- Trained PRESCIENT model checkpoint (PyTorch, e.g. 2-layer 400-unit model from veres-fate.sh, 5 seeds)
- Normalized gene expression with time-point labels (day 0–6) and cell type annotations for Veres et al. 2019 in vitro β-cell differentiation (.csv/.tsv/.txt/.h5ad)

## Outputs

- perturbation_temporal_results.csv — endocrine-fate outcome shifts per perturbation day (0–6) for NKX6.1 and NEUROG3 overexpression
- perturbation_subpopulation_results.csv — fate-bias shifts toward β-, α-, and EC-cell outcomes per starting subpopulation × TF set (early: NKX2.2/NKX6.1/PAX4/ARX; late: PDX1/MAFA/MAFB/ISL1)

## How to apply

Load the PRESCIENT data_pt torch object (serialized dict with data, celltype, genes, tps, x, xp, pca, y) and a trained PRESCIENT model checkpoint with PyTorch. For each day 0–6, initialize simulations from the x tensors split by timepoint and apply overexpression (e.g. z = 10 for NKX6.1 and NEUROG3) to initial cell states, running simulations in PCA space and projecting through the stored sklearn PCA and umap.UMAP objects to quantify endocrine-fate shifts. Separately, initialize from each starting subpopulation (SOX2+, NKX6.1+, neurog3-early, neurog3-mid, neurog3-late) and apply the early-acting TF set (NKX2.2, NKX6.1, PAX4, ARX) versus the late-acting set (PDX1, MAFA, MAFB, ISL1). Compute fate-bias shifts toward β-, α-, and EC-cell outcomes per condition; significance is judged at p < 0.05. Write day-resolved results to perturbation_temporal_results.csv and subpopulation-resolved results to perturbation_subpopulation_results.csv. The rationale is that PRESCIENT explicitly supports perturbations of multiple genes at different time points and from different starting populations, so temporal and subpopulation specificity are first-class axes of the analysis.

## Evaluation signals

- Early endocrine-specification TF perturbations (NKX2.2, NKX6.1, PAX4, ARX) significantly increase final β-cell proportion at p < 0.05 when introduced in pancreatic progenitors, with minimal effect further along endocrine induction
- NKX6.1 and NEUROG3 (z = 10) perturbations increase endocrine-cell fractions at day 0, and this effect diminishes monotonically for perturbations introduced at later days 0–6
- PDX1 perturbation increases β-cell fractions in both progenitors and cells with late NEUROG3 expression, unlike the early-acting set
- Early-acting versus late-acting TF sets show distinct, non-identical fate-shift patterns across subpopulations, reproducing the qualitative patterns of Figs. 4d and 5b, c
- Output CSVs are populated with day-resolved (perturbation_temporal_results.csv) and subpopulation-resolved (perturbation_subpopulation_results.csv) rows covering all 7 timepoints and all 5 starting populations

## Limitations

- The model requires the final time point of the dataset to be at steady-state
- Information is lost about individual genes when transforming data into PCA space
- Lowly-expressed genes important to cell fate decisions may be dropped altogether in the scRNA-seq data
- Association of certain genes with specific cell fates does not necessarily imply causality; there remain challenges to confidently suggest gene sets for experimental perturbation
- Model performance depends on growth rates and would likely benefit from better estimates of proliferation
