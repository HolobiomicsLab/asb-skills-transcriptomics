# monte-carlo-bias-mse-evaluation

## Summary

A Monte Carlo simulation benchmark that generates synthetic gene-pair UMI counts with a known latent correlation (true rho = 0.5) via a Gaussian copula over negative-binomial marginals, then quantifies each co-expression estimator's bias and mean squared error against expression level, as used to show CS-CORE avoids mean-correlation bias.

## When to use

Use when comparing co-expression estimators on scRNA-seq count data where a ground-truth correlation is known, to measure estimation bias and MSE as functions of gene expression level (e.g., reproducing Fig. 3a / Supplementary Fig. 4).

## When NOT to use

- Real scRNA-seq datasets where the true underlying correlation is unknown — bias and MSE against ground truth cannot be computed, so reproducibility or pathway enrichment analyses are the appropriate alternatives
- The goal is differential co-expression testing (e.g., diff_CSCORE donor-level permutation) rather than accuracy evaluation of point correlation estimates
- Evaluating methods on non-UMI-count inputs or distributions outside the Poisson measurement model used in this simulation design

## Inputs

- Calibrated simulation parameters: negative-binomial marginal mean/dispersion and size-factor distribution fitted from a reference scRNA-seq dataset (postmortem brain, Alzheimer's disease patients and controls)
- Synthetic gene-pair UMI count matrices generated by Gaussian copula with true rho = 0.5 over an expression-level grid
- Correlation estimates from CS-CORE (CSCORE/CSCORE_IRLS, post_process_est) and from the ten competing estimators
- True latent correlation value (rho = 0.5) used as the reference target

## Outputs

- Per-method bias curves of correlation estimates as a function of expression level (reproducing Fig. 3a)
- Per-method mean squared error curves against expression level (Supplementary Fig. 4)
- Aggregated results table simulation_bias_mse.csv

## How to apply

First calibrate negative-binomial marginal mean/dispersion parameters and the size-factor distribution from a reference scRNA-seq dataset — in this study, the postmortem brain scRNA-seq data of Alzheimer's disease patients and controls (ref. 17). Generate synthetic gene-pair UMI counts via a Gaussian copula with true latent correlation rho = 0.5 over a grid of expression levels, passing latent expression through a Poisson measurement process with varying sequencing depths. Run CS-CORE (CSCORE/CSCORE_IRLS with IRLS estimation and post_process_est) plus the ten competing estimators (Pearson and Spearman on UMI counts, rho-sctransform, rho-analytic PR, locCSN, NoiseRegularization, normalisr, etc.) on each replicate. Compute per-method bias and MSE of correlation estimates against the true rho at every expression level, plot per-method bias/MSE curves, and write aggregated results to simulation_bias_mse.csv. The decision point: a method is judged accurate if its bias curve lies near zero with low MSE across the expression grid; methods whose bias spuriously increases with expression level are subject to mean-correlation bias.


## Evaluation signals

- CS-CORE's bias curve lies near zero across the full grid of expression levels, consistent with the reported finding that CS-CORE was not subject to mean-correlation bias
- CS-CORE achieves the smallest mean squared errors among all eleven estimators (Supplementary Fig. 4)
- Competing estimators show bias that most severely underestimates co-expressions for low/moderate expression genes and spuriously increases with expression levels
- simulation_bias_mse.csv contains per-method bias and MSE values at every expression level against true rho = 0.5
- Simulated counts retain the calibrated negative-binomial marginals and the size-factor distribution of the Alzheimer's postmortem brain reference dataset

## Limitations

- CS-CORE's measurement model uses only a Poisson distribution; adaptation to other count distributions such as the negative binomial was deferred to future work, so the simulation design's NB marginals probe an assumption CS-CORE itself does not fully model
- Simulation assumes gene expressions from cells of the same cell type follow the same distribution, which may not hold across individuals with different genetic, demographic and clinical characteristics
- Baseline set is specific (ten estimators including locCSN, NoiseRegularization, normalisr, rho-sctransform, rho-analytic PR); results do not cover methods outside this list
