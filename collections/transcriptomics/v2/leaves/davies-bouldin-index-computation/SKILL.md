# davies-bouldin-index-computation

## Summary

Computes the Davies–Bouldin index (DBI) to quantify clustering quality of synthetic scRNA-seq data, benchmarked against the DBI of Seurat clustering on the original data. Used in the scDBM/scVI pilot-study pipeline to judge how faithfully synthetic datasets reproduce ground-truth cluster structure.

## When to use

Use when evaluating clustering quality of generated or sub-sampled single-cell datasets against a ground-truth partition (e.g., synthetic scRNA-seq from pilot studies) using a compactness/separation metric.

## When NOT to use

- The goal is batch-effect correction or batch-effect-aware generation — the study explicitly examined all models without batch correction capabilities, and scDBMs currently cannot learn batch effects
- Only per-gene distribution fidelity (medians, IQRs, coefficients of variation, zero-count proportions) is of interest rather than cluster-level quality — a separate MAD-per-gene evaluation is used for that
- The input lacks a ground-truth or reference cluster partition from the original data; DBI on synthetic labels alone cannot be benchmarked against the original-data Seurat clustering

## Inputs

- Preprocessed original scRNA-seq count matrix (PBMC4k, 10x Genomics; Segerstolpe, Smart-seq2) with Seurat cluster labels
- Synthetic datasets generated by scDBM (Gibbs sampling), scVI-prior, scVI-posterior, or the negative-binomial noise baseline for each pilot size and replicate
- UMAP embeddings of the original data used for nearest-neighbor cluster-label assignment

## Outputs

- Per-replicate DBI values for each model, pilot size, and dataset, with the reference DBI of the original Seurat clustering
- Aggregated tidy table (dbi_ari_results.csv) containing DBI and ARI across models, pilot sizes, and 30 replicates, including 25th/75th percentile hinges

## How to apply

First apply UMAP dimensionality reduction and Seurat clustering to the original data (e.g., PBMC4k or Segerstolpe) and record the reference DBI of this Seurat partition, which serves as the benchmark (the orange dotted line in Figure 2). Assign each synthetic observation the cluster label of its nearest original observation by minimizing Euclidean distance in UMAP space, then compute the DBI on each synthetic cluster partition. Repeat for every generative model (scDBM, scVI-prior, scVI-posterior, and the negative-binomial noise baseline built from gamma scale/shape parameters drawn from Uniform(0,5) and Uniform(0,10) used as Poisson rate parameters) across the six pilot sizes (384, 768, 1152, 1536, 1920, 2304 cells) and 30 replicates per size. Pair each DBI value with the adjusted Rand index (ARI) between synthetic and original labels to capture clustering similarity from both perspectives. Aggregate the results (including 25th/75th percentile boxplot hinges) into a tidy CSV (dbi_ari_results.csv) so variability across replicates can be compared between models and technologies.

## Related tools

- **UMAP** (Dimensionality reduction of original data; synthetic observations are mapped to original observations by minimizing Euclidean distance in UMAP space to acquire cluster labels before DBI computation)
- **Seurat** (Clustering of the original data to obtain ground-truth cluster labels and the reference (orange-line) DBI for benchmarking)
- **scVI** (Generative model producing synthetic datasets from pilot sub-samples in prior and posterior variants; the posterior variant's DBI variability is the key diagnostic) — https://github.com/scverse/scvi-tools
- **scDBM** (Deep Boltzmann machine trained on pilot sub-samples; synthetic data generated via Gibbs sampling and evaluated with DBI/ARI) — https://github.com/MTreppner/scDBM.jl

## Evaluation signals

- The reference DBI of the original Seurat clustering (the orange dotted line in Figure 2) is computed and included for benchmarking alongside all synthetic-data DBI values
- DBI and ARI are aggregated over all models, six pilot sizes (384–2304 cells), and 30 replicates per size, with 25th/75th percentile hinges, and written to dbi_ari_results.csv
- The scVI-posterior variant exhibits the highest DBI/ARI variability across replicates at small pilot sizes, consistent with amplification of artifacts of small datasets — sometimes performing worse than the noisy-pilot baseline
- Segerstolpe (Smart-seq2) DBI distributions are tighter and closer to the original-data DBI than the sparser PBMC4k (10x Genomics) distributions, matching the reported technology-sparsity finding

## Limitations

- The metric relies on the unverified assumption that synthetic cells mapped to the original data are mapped to the correct clusters
- scVI-posterior sampling can amplify artifacts of small pilot datasets, producing high variability in clustering quality and sometimes performing worse than the negative-binomial noise baseline
- Evaluating synthetic data quality via DBI alone can be ambiguous; the paper combines DBI with ARI, cluster-proportion differences, and per-gene MADs to mitigate this
