# cite-seq-protein-mrna-correlation-analysis

## Summary

Benchmark DCA scRNA-seq denoising against CITE-seq protein measurements by using per-cell antibody-derived-tag (ADT) counts as ground truth for mRNA, quantifying protein-RNA Spearman correlation gains and CD3E detection-rate increases on the Stoeckius et al. cord blood dataset.

## When to use

Use when evaluating whether a denoising/imputation method improves scRNA-seq data using CITE-seq ADT protein abundances as ground truth, e.g. reproducing Fig. 6 protein-mRNA co-expression and CD3E detection-rate analyses.

## When NOT to use

- Input data lacks matched per-cell protein (ADT) measurements, so no protein ground truth exists for the correlation comparison
- The RNA counts were already size-factor normalized — DCA expects raw counts and an NB noise model without normalization
- The goal is denoising for downstream clustering/visualization rather than quantitative validation against an independent modality

## Inputs

- Stoeckius et al. CITE-seq cord blood count matrix (GSE100866) with RNA counts and ADT protein measurements
- Cell annotation of T cells for the CD3E detection-rate comparison

## Outputs

- protein_mrna_correlations.csv containing per-pair Spearman correlations (original vs DCA-denoised) and CD3E detection fractions
- CD3E detection fraction per condition (expected ~80% original vs ~99.9% denoised)

## How to apply

Load the Stoeckius et al. CITE-seq cord blood count matrix (GSE100866) with anndata/Scanpy and subset to the 8,005 human cells by removing cells with less than 90% human UMI counts, pairing RNA counts with the ADT protein measurements. Run DCA (Keras/TensorFlow backend) on the raw RNA counts using the negative binomial (NB) noise model without size-factor normalization; the denoised matrix is generated by replacing original counts with the predicted NB mean. Compute Spearman rank correlations between protein and mRNA abundances for the eight protein-mRNA pairs (e.g. CD3, CD4, CD8, CD127) across all cells, separately for original and denoised data, and report the median across pairs. Additionally compute the fraction of T cells with non-zero CD3E mRNA counts before and after denoising, using the CD3 protein measurement as ground truth. Write per-pair correlations and detection fractions to protein_mrna_correlations.csv; acceptance is higher post-DCA correlations and CD3E detection rising from ~80% to ~99.9%.

- **DCA** (Deep count autoencoder used to denoise RNA counts with NB noise model, replacing counts with the predicted NB mean) — https://github.com/theislab/dca
- **anndata** (Loads and stores the CITE-seq RNA and ADT count matrix in h5ad format) — https://github.com/theislab/anndata

## Examples

```
dca(adata, ae_type='nb-conddisp'); scipy.stats.spearmanr(protein['CD3'], adata[:, 'CD3E'].X)
```

## Evaluation signals

- Median Spearman correlation across the eight protein-mRNA pairs is higher after DCA denoising than on the original counts
- CD3E mRNA is detected (non-zero) in ~99.9% of T cells after DCA vs ~80% originally, moving toward the CD3 protein ground truth
- The results table protein_mrna_correlations.csv contains entries for all eight pairs under both original and denoised conditions
- Denoised matrix is the predicted NB mean, not raw counts, confirming the denoising step was run as specified

## Limitations

- Evaluation of denoising is difficult because defining a ground truth for real data is challenging; here per-cell CITE-seq protein measurements supply it
- It may be difficult to determine in general when denoising improves scRNA-seq data
- Overimputation can occur with inadequate hyperparameter choices such as too low-dimensional a bottleneck layer, and hyperparameters were not fine-tuned for the manuscript's analyses
- No DOI, data accession IDs, or preserved archive (e.g., Zenodo) is provided for the datasets or code
