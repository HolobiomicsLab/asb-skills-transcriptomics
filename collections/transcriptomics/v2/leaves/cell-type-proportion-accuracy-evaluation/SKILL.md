# cell-type-proportion-accuracy-evaluation

## Summary

Benchmark approach for evaluating how accurately Scanpro controls false positives in cell-type proportion analysis, using simulated null datasets and bootstrapped pseudo-replicates, and for selecting the optimal pseudo-replicate count as a function of total cells per condition.

## When to use

Decision support for validating false-positive control of Scanpro proportion analysis on null simulations, and for choosing pseudo-replicate counts (2–14) based on total cell counts per condition (~1,000 to ~50,000 cells).

## When NOT to use

- Datasets where clusters are supported by less than 2% of all cells — the article's benchmarking found such clusters produce unstable results
- Handling rare cell types — bootstrapping has limited benefits for rare cell types per the benchmarking and real-dataset results
- Clusters that may be defined by technical parameters (e.g. batch effects from different sequencing machines or timepoints) rather than true cell types — PA interpretation requires care before trusting accuracy estimates

## Inputs

- Null-simulation datasets generated by sim_scanpro (two conditions, five clusters, ~20,000 cells per condition, 100 datasets per replicate count from 2 to 14)
- AnnData objects (e.g. scanpro_tutorial.h5ad) with sample, cluster, and condition column annotations
- Simulated replicate datasets at escalating total cell counts per condition (~1,000 to ~50,000 cells)

## Outputs

- null_simulation_accuracy.csv with accuracy values per replicate count and pseudo-replicate count
- Sensitivity/FPR, auROC-vs-mean-cell-count, and accuracy benchmark panels (e.g. fig3_benchmark_panels.png)
- Differential proportion calls from the exported anova and t_test routines with sensitivity and false-positive rates versus simulated ground truth

## How to apply

Generate null-simulation datasets with Scanpro's exported sim_scanpro function — 100 datasets per replicate count from 2 to 14, each with two conditions and five clusters whose proportions are unchanged, per-cluster cell counts drawn from a negative-binomial distribution (mu=20000, dispersion=20) yielding ~20,000 cells per condition. Run the scanpro function (from scanpro.scanpro import scanpro) on each replicated dataset, passing the sample, cluster, and condition column annotations; for unreplicated data, use Scanpro's iteration-based bootstrapping to create pseudo-replicates and rerun scanpro with increasing pseudo-replicate counts (2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 10, 14) under both logit and arcsin-sqrt variance-stabilizing transformations. Score accuracy as the fraction of truly unchanged clusters correctly called non-significant, aggregated over the 100 simulated datasets per setting, and call the underlying anova/t_test routines to record sensitivity and false-positive rates against simulated ground truth. Compute auROC as a function of mean total cell count per condition and plot sensitivity/FPR, auROC, and accuracy panels via out.plot (boxplot kind where appropriate). Validate against the reported behavior: replicated accuracy 0.98–0.99; unreplicated accuracy matching the replicated result from 8 pseudo-replicates upward; 2 and 4 pseudo-replicates give the best auROC up to ~5,000 cells, while 8, 10 and 14 perform better above ~25,000 cells.

## Evaluation signals

- Replicated null-simulation accuracy falls in the 0.98–0.99 range, indicating good false-positive control
- Unreplicated (pseudo-replicate) accuracy matches the replicated result from 8 pseudo-replicates upward
- auROC curves show best performance for 2 and 4 pseudo-replicates up to ~5,000 mean cells per condition and for 8–14 pseudo-replicates above ~25,000 cells
- Sensitivity and false-positive rates against simulated ground truth are computed from anova/t_test calls and aggregated over all 100 simulated datasets per setting
- Output files (null_simulation_accuracy.csv, fig3_benchmark_panels.png) are produced with per-replicate-count and per-pseudo-replicate-count resolution

## Limitations

- Clusters supported by less than 2% of all cells in an experiment produce unstable results
- Bootstrapping has limited benefits when handling rare cell types
- Bootstrapping-based results should be interpreted with caution and require additional experimental validation
- Clustering parameters such as Leiden resolution critically affect cluster number and size, challenging biologically meaningful proportion analysis
