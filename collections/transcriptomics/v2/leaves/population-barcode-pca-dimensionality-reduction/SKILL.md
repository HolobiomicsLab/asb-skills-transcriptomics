# population-barcode-pca-dimensionality-reduction

## Summary

Principal component analysis (prcomp) on log-normalized population-level barcode counts tables to assess whether clonal composition separates samples by treatment, time course, or condition, as demonstrated for DMSO, IBET-151, and AraC in the bartools dose-escalation workflow.

## When to use

Use when a normalized barcode counts table (e.g. counts/all_counts_combined.tsv) is available and you need to test whether treatments, doses, or time points are distinguishable by global clonal composition, or to check replicate concordance before downstream diversity or clustering analyses.

## When NOT to use

- Input is raw FASTQ or an unnormalized barcode counts table — normalize counts first (upstream BARtab/bartools steps)
- The goal is lineage reconstruction from single-cell expressed barcodes (use lineage reconstruction tools such as Cassiopeia or LinRace, not population-level PCA)
- Samples have no replicate or treatment structure, so PCA separation cannot be interpreted against a biological grouping

## Inputs

- Normalized combined barcode counts table (TSV, e.g. counts/all_counts_combined.tsv) generated by BARtab and processed with bartools
- Sample metadata mapping samples to treatment (DMSO, IBET-151, AraC), dose, time point, and replicate labels

## Outputs

- PCA ordination of samples (PC1 vs PC2 scores) colored by treatment
- Diversity trends across time points (Shannon, Simpson, Gini indices per sample)
- Pairwise Pearson correlation matrix between biological replicates
- Hierarchical clustering dendrogram of samples based on correlation dissimilarity of barcode composition

## How to apply

Load the normalized combined barcode counts table (counts/all_counts_combined.tsv) in R with tidyverse, log-transform (e.g. log-normalized barcode counts), and run prcomp with samples as rows and barcodes as features. Plot PC1 versus PC2 colored by treatment (DMSO, IBET-151, AraC) to visually test whether treatments separate along PC1 and whether a dose-escalation time course separates along PC2. Complement PCA with per-sample Shannon, Simpson, and Gini diversity indices (via bartools functions) and pairwise Pearson correlations between biological replicates, requiring correlations > 0.95 for DMSO and IBET-151. Hierarchical clustering on correlation dissimilarity can then confirm that distinct conditions (e.g. AraC IC90) form an independent cluster from the gradual dose-escalation samples.

## Related tools

- **bartools** (R package providing downstream population-level barcode analysis functions, including diversity indices and workflows used to normalize and analyze the dose-escalation counts table prior to PCA) — https://github.com/DaneVass/bartools
- **R** (Environment in which prcomp PCA, diversity computation, Pearson correlations, and hierarchical clustering are performed with tidyverse)
- **BARtab** (Nextflow pipeline that generates the barcode counts table (counts/all_counts_combined.tsv) that serves as PCA input) — https://github.com/DaneVass/BARtab
- **tidyverse** (R package collection used to load, normalize, and reshape the barcode counts table before ordination and plotting)

## Examples

```
pca <- prcomp(t(log2(counts + 1))); plot(pca$x[,1], pca$x[,2], col = as.factor(meta$treatment))
```

## Evaluation signals

- Treatments separate along PC1 in the PCA plot (DMSO vs IBET-151 vs AraC)
- The dose-escalation time course separates along PC2
- Pairwise Pearson correlations between biological replicates for DMSO and IBET-151 all exceed 0.95
- The dendrogram places AraC IC90 samples in a distinct cluster independent of the gradual dose-escalation samples
- Replicate samples within a condition show similar overall clonal composition in the ordination

## Limitations

- No accepted gold-standard dataset with known barcode abundance exists for benchmarking downstream population-level analyses
- Clustering using a default Levenshtein distance of 3 is conservative to avoid collapsing truly distinct barcodes; inappropriate thresholds can distort barcode-level structure feeding PCA
- PCA on log-normalized counts assumes prior adequate filtering of low-support barcodes (e.g. UMI-based filters); noisy rare barcodes can obscure treatment separation
