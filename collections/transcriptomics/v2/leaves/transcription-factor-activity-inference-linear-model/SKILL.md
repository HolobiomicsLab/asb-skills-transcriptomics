# transcription-factor-activity-inference-linear-model

> **License: noncommercial** — confirm your use is a permitted (noncommercial) purpose before applying; commercial use requires a separate license (see `metadata.tool_license`). <!-- asb-license-banner -->
## Summary

Infers dynamic transcription factor activities from single-cell RNA-seq by extending the ISMARA linear model of gene regulation (Balwierz et al. 2014) to single-cell transcriptomics with data-diffusion regularization, along the stochastic differentiation trajectories generated by FateCompass. It enables time- and lineage-specific TF activity estimation where TF activities cannot be measured directly, only gene expression.

## When to use

Use when you have preprocessed single-cell RNA-seq (Scanpy/scVelo adata) plus a TF binding-site matrix and want to estimate time- and fate-specific TF activities along stochastic cell-fate differentiation trajectories.

## When NOT to use

- The input is raw unprocessed scRNA-seq without basic Scanpy/scVelo preprocessing and cluster annotations
- You need distal regulatory interactions — FateCompass only considers binding sites in promoter regions, not long-range interactions
- Trajectories are already fixed or the analysis is bulk RNA-seq only, where the original ISMARA suffices without the single-cell trajectory machinery

## Inputs

- Preprocessed AnnData object (adata) with cell type/cluster annotations and optionally an RNA velocity field
- TF binding-site matrix (columns = TFs or motifs, rows = expressed genes)
- Stochastic differentiation trajectories from the FateCompass differentiation_trajectories.m step

## Outputs

- Time- and fate-specific TF activity estimates from the linear model of gene regulation
- Bootstrap variability estimates of TF activities across the trajectory ensemble
- Differential TF activity classification (lineage-specific TFs by the three statistical criteria, e.g., differential_tf_activity_results.csv)

## How to apply

First obtain stochastic differentiation trajectories from a FateCompass Markov-process step (driven by RNA velocity or differentiation potential). Load a TF binding-site matrix whose columns are the TFs/motifs and whose rows are the expressed genes. Model observed gene expression as a linear combination of regulatory sites and TF activities, estimating time- and fate-specific TF activities via the tf_activities step (implemented in Matlab for the published pancreatic E15.5 reproduction; the FateCompass module itself is fatecompass.py in Python). Run bootstrapping.m across the trajectory ensemble to quantify the variability of the estimated TF activities. Judge correctness by the differential TF activity analysis applying three criteria: Z-score significance of the TF to explain variability of the linear model, change of regulatory activity throughout the cell-fate decision, and dynamical correlation of TF activity with the TF mRNA level (e.g., the published analysis recovered 86 lineage-specific TFs — 22 both-fate, 38 beta-specific, 25 alpha-specific — with Bcl11a having the highest Z score, 4.1192).

## Related tools

## Examples

```
matlab -batch "run('tf_activities.m')"
```

## Evaluation signals

- Counts of classified lineage-specific TFs match the published decomposition: 86 TFs total (22 specific for both alpha and beta fates, 38 beta-specific, 25 alpha-specific) on the mouse E15.5 pancreatic dataset
- The TF with the highest Z score for explaining the variance of the linear model is Bcl11a with Z = 4.1192
- All three differential criteria are applied: Z-score significance, change of regulatory activity throughout the cell-fate decision, and dynamical correlation of TF activity with TF mRNA level
- Bootstrapping output shows quantified variability of TF activities across the trajectory ensemble (not a single deterministic run)
- Known lineage-specific pancreatic regulators (e.g., alpha- and beta-specific factors, EC-specific CDX2) are recapitulated among the identified regulators

## Limitations

- FateCompass estimates TF activities using only computationally predicted binding sites in promoter regions, ignoring long-range/distal regulatory interactions, which biases the gene-regulatory network structure
- Pipeline input parameters must be hand tuned (detailed tutorials are provided to guide parameter setup)
- Whether putative driver factors identified by the activity analysis are actually involved in endocrine cell differentiation requires further experimental validation
