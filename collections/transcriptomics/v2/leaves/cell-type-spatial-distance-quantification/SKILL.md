# cell-type-spatial-distance-quantification

## Summary

Quantifies pairwise distances between cell types (e.g., myeloid vs. alveolar/airway epithelial cells) in a reconstructed pseudo-space to detect condition-specific spatial reorganization, as demonstrated for COVID-19 immune infiltration using scSpace.

## When to use

Use when scRNA-seq cells have been projected into a spatially informed pseudo-space and you need to test whether the spatial proximity between annotated cell types differs between biological conditions (e.g., normal vs. disease).

## When NOT to use

- When the input is raw ST data rather than projected single-cell coordinates — spatial domain identification methods like SpaGCN, BayesSpace, or DR-SC target ST data, whereas this skill operates on scRNA-seq cells placed in pseudo-space
- When tissues have low spatial coherence (e.g., SCC Patients 5 and 9 in the paper), since the reconstruction underlying the distances may be unreliable

## Inputs

- Projected pseudo-space coordinates for scRNA-seq cells from each condition (control and COVID-19 lung), generated by the scSpace MLP projector
- Cell-type annotations (e.g., myeloid cells, alveolar/airway epithelial cells) from the space-informed Leiden clustering
- Condition labels distinguishing control versus COVID-19 cells

## Outputs

- Normalized pairwise cell-type-to-cell-type distance distributions per condition (myeloid-to-epithelial distances in control vs. COVID-19)
- Statistical comparison of distance reduction in the disease condition
- Identified spatially distinct subpopulations (e.g., MDM and TMDM macrophage clusters)

## How to apply

After projecting scRNA-seq cells into the pseudo-space (TCA-based batch correction against a 10X Visium ST reference, followed by MLP projection), annotate cells by type and cluster spatially with Leiden (leidenalg 0.9.0 / igraph 0.10.2) to resolve spatially variable subpopulations. Compute normalized pairwise pseudo-space distances between cell-type groups — here myeloid cells versus alveolar/airway epithelial cells — separately for each condition (control and COVID-19 lung scRNA-seq). Compare the distance distributions between conditions; a significant reduction in the COVID-19 group indicates spatial infiltration of myeloid cells toward epithelial compartments. Within the space-informed clusters, identify disease-associated subpopulations such as MDM and TMDM macrophages to attribute the distance change to specific subtypes.

## Related tools

- **scSpace** (Reconstructs the pseudo-space of scRNA-seq cells via TCA batch correction and MLP projection so that cell-type spatial distances can be computed) — https://github.com/ZJUFanLab/scSpace

## Examples

```
import scanpy as sc; sc.pp.normalize_total(adata); scSpace_project(adata_st, adata_sc); leiden_clusters = sc.tl.leiden(adata, resolution=1.0); dist = pairwise_pseudo_distance(adata, 'myeloid', 'epithelial', groupby='condition')
```

## Evaluation signals

- Normalized myeloid-to-epithelial pseudo-space distances are significantly smaller in the COVID-19 group than in the control, reproducing the Fig. 6a-k pattern
- MDM and TMDM macrophage subpopulations form distinct clusters in the space-informed Leiden clustering
- Distance distributions are computed on the same normalized scale across conditions so the comparison is valid
- Clusters correspond to spatially variable subpopulations with distinct transcriptional specificities, as expected from scSpace's space-informed cell typing

## Limitations

- The choice of spatial reference directly influences the spatial reconstruction; an appropriateness assessment of the reference (e.g., normal lung Visium for COVID-19 lung scRNA-seq) is required
- Intrinsic differences in spatial homogeneity between tissues may influence the accuracy of scSpace and thus of the distance measurements
- Spatially heterogeneous subpopulations are difficult to identify by traditional single-cell pipelines due to transcriptome similarity, so distances depend on the quality of the space-informed clustering
