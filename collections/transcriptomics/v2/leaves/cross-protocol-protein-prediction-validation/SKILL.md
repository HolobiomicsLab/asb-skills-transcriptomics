# cross-protocol-protein-prediction-validation

## Summary

Benchmark whether a cTP-net model trained on one tissue or assay protocol (e.g., CITE-CBMC or CITE-seq) generalizes to imputing surface protein abundances on a different tissue (PBMC) or protocol (REAP-seq), by comparing cross-cell Pearson correlations of imputed proteins against SAVER-X-denoised RNA baselines.

## When to use

Use when testing transfer of a trained protein-imputation model to data whose cell types are not perfectly aligned with training, or generated by a different antibody-tagging protocol (CITE-seq vs REAP-seq).

## When NOT to use

- Input expression is TPM/RPKM or otherwise not UMI-based count data — cTP-net can only apply to count-based expression input (UMI-based), and CITE-seq data with TPM/RPKM metrics were not available for testing
- Target cells are outside the hematopoietic lineage — the article states imputation of these immune-related markers has not been tried on non-hematopoietic cells
- Evaluating same-population within-sample accuracy rather than cross-tissue/cross-protocol transfer — in that case the same-population trained model is the proper comparison, and it is slightly more accurate than cross-tissue imputation

## Inputs

- CITE-PBMC paired transcriptome–protein dataset (Supplementary Table 1)
- CITE-CBMC paired transcriptome–protein dataset (Supplementary Table 1)
- REAP-PBMC paired transcriptome–protein dataset (Supplementary Table 1)
- SAVER-X-denoised, LogNormalize-transformed UMI count matrices
- Relative-abundance-transformed (geometric mean log-ratio) ADT protein count vectors

## Outputs

- Cross-cell Pearson correlation table (imputed vs measured protein) per protein for each train/test direction
- Comparison of imputation correlations vs denoised RNA correlations, reproducing Fig. 2a and Supplementary Figs. 3b, 4a
- Cross-tissue (PBMC↔CBMC) and cross-protocol (CITE-seq↔REAP-seq) generalization tables

## How to apply

Load the paired transcriptome–protein datasets from Supplementary Table 1 (CITE-PBMC, CITE-CBMC, REAP-PBMC), denoise scRNA-seq counts with SAVER-X using default parameters, then transform denoised counts with Seurat default LogNormalize. Transform surface protein ADT counts with the relative abundance (geometric mean log-ratio) transformation from Stoeckius et al. to form training response vectors. Train the cTP-net multiple-branch neural network with Adam (learning rate 10e-5, 139 epochs) on CITE-CBMC and impute CITE-PBMC proteins, then reverse the roles; fill genes present in training but absent from the testing data with zeros to align feature spaces. Apply the CITE-seq-trained model to REAP-PBMC for cross-protocol evaluation. Compute cross-cell Pearson correlations between imputed and measured protein abundances per protein, reproducing Fig. 2a and Supplementary Figs. 3b, 4a. Accept the result when imputation correlations over measured proteins markedly exceed those of denoised RNA alone — SAVER-X denoising alone contributes only ~0.02 correlation gain, so cTP-net output must clearly surpass that baseline; cross-tissue imputation should improve markedly over denoised RNA though remain slightly less accurate than same-population training.

## Related tools

- **cTP-net** (Multiple-branch neural network trained on one dataset and applied to impute 24 immunophenotype marker proteins on the cross-tissue/cross-protocol test data) — https://github.com/zhouzilu/cTPnet
- **SAVER-X** (Denoises scRNA-seq counts with default parameters before imputation; contributes ~0.02 correlation gain and serves as a baseline in the validation)
- **Seurat** (Applies default LogNormalize transformation to SAVER-X denoised counts to form the network training input)
- **Adam (optimizer)** (Optimizes the cTP-net objective stochastically with learning rate 10e-5 for 139 epochs during cross-tissue training)

## Evaluation signals

- Cross-cell Pearson correlations of imputed proteins on the held-out tissue/protocol markedly exceed correlations of SAVER-X-denoised RNA of the corresponding genes
- The observed gain over denoised RNA clearly exceeds the ~0.02 correlation attributable to SAVER-X denoising alone
- Cross-tissue imputation (CBMC-trained model on PBMC and vice versa) is slightly less accurate than same-population training but still markedly better than denoised RNA
- Genes present in training but absent from testing data are filled with zeros, so per-protein correlations are computed over measured proteins only
- Results reproduce the ordering shown in Fig. 2a and Supplementary Figs. 3b, 4a

## Limitations

- Cross-tissue imputation is slightly less accurate than imputation from a model trained on the same population
- cTP-net can only apply to count-based expression input (UMI-based); accuracy on TPM/RPKM data is unknown
- Generalization was only tested within hematopoietic immune cells; non-hematopoietic cell types were not tested
- Comparisons are limited to the same cells that were in the validation set in the holdout scheme to account for variations between subsets
