# synthetic-data-quality-evaluation

## Summary

A multi-metric evaluation workflow for judging whether synthetic scRNA-seq data generated by deep generative models (scDBM, scVI prior/posterior) from small pilot studies faithfully reflects ground-truth data, using the Davies–Bouldin index (DBI), adjusted Rand index (ARI), cluster-proportion differences, and per-gene distribution statistics, including the technology-stratified comparison of sparser 10x Genomics (PBMC4k) versus Smart-seq2 (Segerstolpe) data.

## When to use

Use when you have synthetic single-cell RNA-seq data generated by scDBM or scVI (prior/posterior variants) from pilot sub-samples (e.g., 384–2304 cells, 30 repetitions) and need to quantify clustering fidelity, cluster-proportion bias, per-gene distribution agreement, and technology-specific generalization limits.

## When NOT to use

- When the task requires batch-effect handling — the article explicitly examined all models without batch correction, and scDBMs currently cannot learn batch effects
- When the pilot sample is not representative of the larger dataset (e.g., experimental settings changed between pilot and full study), since conclusions from small samples require representativeness
- When data are already feature tables or aggregated profiles rather than cell-level count matrices requiring UMAP/Seurat clustering for label mapping

## Inputs

- Ground-truth scRNA-seq count matrices with Seurat-derived cluster labels for PBMC4k (10x Genomics) and Segerstolpe (Smart-seq2)
- Synthetic datasets generated by scDBM (Gibbs sampling) and scVI (prior and posterior sampling) from 30 sub-samples per pilot size (384–2304 cells)
- Negative binomial noise baseline samples (gamma scale Uniform(0,5), shape Uniform(0,10) used as Poisson rates)
- UMAP embeddings of original and synthetic data

## Outputs

- DBI and ARI values per model, dataset, and pilot size (30 repetitions each), compared against the reference Seurat DBI on original data
- Mean absolute differences in cell counts per cluster (Table 1-style stratified table)
- Mean absolute deviation per gene for medians, interquartile ranges, coefficients of variation, and zero-count proportions
- Technology comparison table (technology_comparison.csv) contrasting 10x Genomics vs Smart-seq2 inference quality

## How to apply

Sub-sample the original PBMC4k (10x Genomics) and Segerstolpe (Smart-seq2) datasets at pilot sizes of 384, 768, 1152, 1536, 1920, and 2304 cells, repeated 30 times per size, train scDBM (Gibbs sampling) and scVI (prior and posterior sampling), and generate synthetic data at the original study size. Apply UMAP dimensionality reduction and Seurat clustering to the original data, then acquire cluster labels for synthetic observations by mapping each synthetic cell to its closest original-data observation via Euclidean distance in UMAP space. Compute DBI and ARI for each model (plus a negative binomial noise baseline with gamma-drawn scale Uniform(0,5) and shape Uniform(0,10) used as Poisson rates), the mean absolute difference in cell counts per cluster between synthetic and original data (mirroring Table 1), and the mean absolute deviation per gene for medians, interquartile ranges, coefficients of variation, and proportion of zero counts. Finally, contrast the variability of scVI posterior DBI/ARI on the sparse PBMC4k data against the tighter, ground-truth-closer DBI distributions on Segerstolpe to encode that higher sparsity makes small-to-large inference harder.

## Evaluation signals

- The tidy output table has one row per model-dataset-pilot-size combination with non-negative cluster-count differences
- Tabulated Table 1 values match the paper's reported numbers, and DBI/ARI trends qualitatively match Figure 2 (higher variability for scVI posterior on PBMC4k, tighter distributions closer to the Seurat reference DBI on Segerstolpe)
- Mean absolute cluster-count differences decrease (or near-monotonically decrease) with increasing pilot size from 384 to 2304 cells, reflecting convergence of cluster proportions toward ground truth
- Synthetic data reproduce the univariate distribution of most genes, with residual problems confined to bimodally expressed genes
- Technology comparison reproduces the finding that all models show less variable, ground-truth-closer DBIs on Segerstolpe (Smart-seq2) than on PBMC4k (10x Genomics)

## Limitations

- The evaluation relies on the unverified assumption that synthetic cells mapped to the original data in UMAP space are mapped to the correct clusters
- Evaluating synthetic data quality is difficult, with sometimes ambiguous conclusions based on multiple quality measures
- Batch effects were not investigated; all models were examined without potentially available batch correction capabilities, and scDBMs cannot learn batch effects
- Generative-model approaches overrepresent highly abundant cell types and miss less abundant ones, so cluster-proportion metrics will not reach zero even at large pilot sizes
- A statistical-test-based method for evaluating latent representation quality (resampling null distributions) was still under development and not available
