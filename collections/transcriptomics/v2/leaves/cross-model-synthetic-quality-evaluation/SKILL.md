# cross-model-synthetic-quality-evaluation

## Summary

Evaluate how faithfully synthetic scRNA-seq datasets generated by scDBM, scVI (prior), and scVI (posterior) reproduce per-gene univariate distributions of the original data, by comparing medians, interquartile ranges, coefficients of variation, and zero-count proportions across pilot sizes of 384–2304 cells.

## When to use

Use when comparing synthetic single-cell RNA-seq data from multiple deep generative models against an original count matrix to assess per-gene univariate distribution fidelity, including bimodality failures.

## When NOT to use

- When evaluating clustering-level properties (ARI, Davies–Bouldin index, cluster proportions) rather than per-gene univariate distributions
- When the synthetic data must preserve batch effects — the paper examined all models without batch correction, and scDBMs currently cannot learn batch effects
- When the input data is not count-level scRNA-seq from the models under comparison (e.g., already-aggregated expression summaries)

## Inputs

- Original scRNA-seq count matrices (PBMC4k 10x Genomics, Segerstolpe Smart-seq2)
- Synthetic count matrices from scDBM, scVI prior, and scVI posterior for each pilot size (384–2304 cells) and 30 sub-sampling replicates
- Gene name and cluster label files (e.g., segerstolpe_gene_names.csv)

## Outputs

- res_mad_descriptive_stats.csv with per-gene MAD values for median, IQR, CV, and zero-count proportion by model and pilot size
- res_bimodality_cd74.png comparing original vs. synthetic CD74 univariate distributions

## How to apply

Load the original count matrices (PBMC4k and Segerstolpe) together with synthetic datasets from scDBM (Gibbs sampling), scVI prior, and scVI posterior, generated from pilot sub-samples of 384, 768, 1152, 1536, 1920, and 2304 cells with 30 replicates per size. For each gene in both original and synthetic data, compute the median, interquartile range, coefficient of variation, and proportion of zero counts. Calculate the mean absolute deviation (MAD) per gene between synthetic and original values for each of the four statistics, aggregating over the 30 replicates, and store the results in res_mad_descriptive_stats.csv ordered by model, pilot size, and metric. To diagnose bimodality failures, plot marginal univariate distributions of exemplary genes such as CD74 from PBMC4k, overlaying original and per-model synthetic densities, and save as res_bimodality_cd74.png. This mirrors the paper's evaluation of distributional fidelity via Supplementary Fig. 3.

## Evaluation signals

- res_mad_descriptive_stats.csv contains all four metrics (median, IQR, CV, zero proportion) for the three models across all six pilot sizes
- MAD values are averaged over the 30 sub-sampling replicates per pilot size and ordered by model, pilot size, and metric
- res_bimodality_cd74.png shows that synthetic distributions of CD74 fail to capture the bimodal pattern present in the original PBMC4k data, consistent with Supplementary Fig. 3
- Most genes show small MADs (distributions learned), while bimodal genes stand out with large deviations in median/IQR/CV
- Zero-count proportion MADs are larger for the sparser 10x (PBMC4k) data than for Smart-seq2 (Segerstolpe), reflecting higher sparsity

## Limitations

- All approaches learn the univariate distribution of most genes but fail to reproduce bimodal gene distributions, exemplified by CD74 in PBMC4k
- Evaluation of synthetic data quality is difficult, with sometimes ambiguous conclusions based on multiple quality measures
- All models were examined without using potentially available batch correction capabilities, and scDBMs currently cannot learn batch effects
- For 10x datasets with higher sparsity, inference from small to larger datasets is more challenging
