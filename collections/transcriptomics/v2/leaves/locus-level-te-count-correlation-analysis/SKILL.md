# locus-level-te-count-correlation-analysis

> **License: noncommercial** — confirm your use is a permitted (noncommercial) purpose before applying; commercial use requires a separate license (see `metadata.tool_license`). <!-- asb-license-banner -->
## Summary

Benchmark locus-specific transposable element (TE) quantification by comparing method-estimated per-locus counts against simulated Polyester ground truth using locus-level R2 correlation and percent of simulated reads recaptured, as done for MATES vs scTE/SoloTE.

## When to use

Use when evaluating a locus-specific TE quantification method (e.g., MATES, scTE, SoloTE) against simulated single-cell data with known ground-truth locus abundances, or when reproducing the controlled-simulation validation reported in a TE quantification paper.

## When NOT to use

- Input is real sequencing data without a simulated ground truth — R2 against ground truth cannot be computed; use orthogonal validation (e.g., long-read read-back) instead
- The TE of interest is a long autonomously expressed element (e.g., L1HS spanning several thousand bases) — MATES' 2001-bp coverage vector limits quantification accuracy
- The goal is subfamily-level rather than locus-specific TE quantification, where simpler counting methods suffice

## Inputs

- Simulated single-cell BAM files generated by Polyester with known ground-truth locus abundances (Alu ~300 bp, simple repeats, HERVK-int TE1/TE2, 100-bp reads)
- TE reference annotations (e.g., TE_full.bed / TE_nooverlap.bed from RepeatMasker-based build_reference.py)
- Simulated long-read (scNanoRNA-seq-like) BAM datasets for TE_quantifier_LongRead

## Outputs

- Locus-level R2 correlation of estimated vs ground-truth TE counts per method
- Percentage of simulated reads recaptured per method (e.g., MATES 96.14% vs scTE 92.57%)
- Isoform-specific count comparisons (HERVK TE1/TE2: 4140/1295 vs ground truth 4126/1282)
- simulation_benchmark_results.csv with all metrics

## How to apply

Generate simulated single-cell reads with Polyester using known ground-truth locus abundances (e.g., 6 Alu subfamilies of ~300 bp, 15 simple-repeat subfamilies, HERVK-int isoform pair TE1/TE2, and 100-bp reads modeled on scNanoRNA-seq data). Process the simulated BAM files with the method under test — for MATES, run bam_processor and data_processor to segregate unique- and multi-mapping reads and build 2001-bp coverage vectors centered on TE start positions, then run MATES_model (TE_quantifier for short reads, TE_quantifier_LongRead for long reads); run baselines (scTE, SoloTE) on identical BAMs. Compare all methods against the Polyester ground truth by computing locus-level R2 correlations, percentage of simulated reads captured per method, and isoform-specific counts (e.g., HERVK TE1/TE2). Write all metrics to a results CSV. Accept the reproduction when MATES reaches Alu R2 = 0.7420 with 96.14% of simulated reads captured (vs scTE 92.57%), simple-repeat locus R2 = 0.7479, HERVK counts 4140/1295 vs ground truth 4126/1282, and long-read locus-level R2 = 0.4923 (vs SoloTE strategy 0.1178).

## Evaluation signals

- Alu locus-level R2 ≈ 0.7420 for MATES vs simulated ground truth
- Percentage of simulated reads captured: MATES ≈ 96.14% vs scTE ≈ 92.57%
- Simple-repeat locus-level R2 ≈ 0.7479
- HERVK TE1/TE2 counts ≈ 4140/1295 against ground truth 4126/1282
- Long-read locus-level R2 ≈ 0.4923 (vs SoloTE strategy 0.1178)

## Limitations

- MATES requires more computational resources (GPUs) than scTE and SoloTE
- Limited ability to quantify long autonomously expressed TEs such as L1HS spanning several thousand bases; estimated multi-mapping rate may be inaccurate for TEs longer than 2001 bp due to incomplete flanking coverage
- Identical TE copies such as HERVK isoforms are hard to distinguish due to high sequence similarity and lack of uniquely mappable flanking regions
- Trimming TE-gene overlaps may unintentionally distort TE quantification, particularly where TEs intersect neighboring genes
