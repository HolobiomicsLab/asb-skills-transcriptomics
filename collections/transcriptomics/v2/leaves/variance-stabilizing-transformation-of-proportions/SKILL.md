# variance-stabilizing-transformation-of-proportions

## Summary

Applies variance-stabilizing transformations (logit and arcsin-sqrt) to cell-type proportion data within Scanpro's proportion analysis, so that per-cluster proportions—especially bootstrapped pseudo-replicates from non-replicated single-cell datasets—meet the assumptions of the downstream ANOVA/t-test statistics.

## When to use

Use when performing differential proportion analysis (PA) on single-cell cluster proportions (AnnData input) and raw per-cluster counts must be variance-stabilized via logit or arcsin-sqrt before ANOVA/t-test calls, particularly when pseudo-replicates are generated by bootstrapping.

## When NOT to use

- Clusters supported by less than 2% of all cells — benchmarking shows such rare cell types produce unstable results and bootstrapping has limited benefits for them
- Data that are not cell-type proportions or per-cluster counts from single-cell resolution data (e.g. already-summarized feature tables unrelated to compositional analysis)

## Inputs

- AnnData object (.h5ad) with clustered cells, e.g. scanpro_tutorial.h5ad
- Column annotations: clusters_col (cell types/clusters), conds_col (conditions), samples_col (replicates; omitted or None triggers bootstrapping)

## Outputs

- Scanpro results object with per-cluster differential proportion statistics (out.results)
- ANOVA/t-test calls on variance-stabilized proportions per cell type
- Result plots (e.g. boxplots) via out.plot()

## How to apply

Start from a clustered and annotated AnnData object where cells are assigned to cell types (clusters_col), conditions (conds_col) and, when replicated, samples (samples_col). Compute per-cluster cell proportions, then apply a variance-stabilizing transformation—Scanpro supports the logit and arcsin-sqrt transforms—to stabilize proportion variance before testing. If samples_col is None or omitted, Scanpro's iteration-based bootstrapping simulates pseudo-replicates, and the transformation choice is evaluated per pseudo-replicate count (e.g., 2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 10, 14 pseudo-replicates); benchmarks indicate 2–4 pseudo-replicates give the best auROC for mean cell counts up to ~5,000 cells per condition, while 8–14 perform better above ~25,000 cells. The transformed proportions are then fed to the exported statistical routines (anova for multi-condition, t_test for two-condition designs). Results are inspected via out.results and visualized with out.plot() (kind='boxplot' where appropriate).


## Evaluation signals

- Benchmark auROC curves should show good performance for 2 and 4 pseudo-replicates up to ~5,000 mean cells per condition, and for 8–14 pseudo-replicates above ~25,000 cells
- Sensitivity and false-positive rates recorded against simulated ground truth (via sim_scanpro-generated replicate datasets) should remain in expected ranges
- Results are retrievable as out.results and plottable via out.plot() without errors
- Clusters with <2% of all cells should be flagged/excluded as unstable per the benchmarking finding

## Limitations

- Bootstrapping has limited benefits when handling rare cell types
- Clusters supported by less than 2% of all cells produce unstable results; prefer larger, less specific clusters with higher cell numbers
- Lack of replicates limits conclusions about variability, and bootstrapping-based results should be interpreted with caution and validated experimentally
