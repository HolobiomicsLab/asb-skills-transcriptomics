# adjusted-rand-index-evaluation

## Summary

Quantifies clustering similarity between synthetic scRNA-seq data and ground-truth labels using the adjusted Rand index (ARI), complementing the Davies–Bouldin index, to judge whether generative models (scDBM, scVI-prior, scVI-posterior) trained on small pilots reproduce cluster structure.

## When to use

Use when you need to score how well cluster structure in synthetic data matches a ground-truth reference clustering, e.g. after mapping synthetic cells onto original-data clusters in UMAP space across pilot sizes.

## When NOT to use

- You have no ground-truth reference clustering — the method relies on the assumption that synthetic cells mapped to the original data are mapped to the correct clusters
- You need to evaluate synthetic data quality without a reference partition (e.g. latent-representation testing), which the authors note is still under development via resampling-based statistical tests
- Your goal is batch correction or generalization of generative models across batches — the authors explicitly examined all models without batch-correction capabilities and excluded batch effects from scope

## Inputs

- Preprocessed original scRNA-seq count matrices (PBMC4k from 10x Genomics; Segerstolpe from Smart-seq2) with ground-truth Seurat cluster labels on UMAP embeddings
- Synthetic count matrices generated by scDBM (Gibbs sampling), scVI prior, scVI posterior, and the negative-binomial noise baseline for 30 sub-samples per pilot size (384–2304 cells)
- Reference DBI of the Seurat clustering on the original data for benchmarking

## Outputs

- Per-model, per-pilot-size, per-replicate ARI values (and DBI values) aggregated with 25th/75th-percentile boxplot hinges, written to dbi_ari_results.csv
- Technology-stratified tidy table (technology, dataset, generative model, metric, value) written to technology_comparison.csv

## How to apply

First obtain ground-truth labels by applying UMAP dimensionality reduction and Seurat clustering to the original dataset (PBMC4k or Segerstolpe). For each synthetic dataset, assign every synthetic observation the cluster label of its nearest original observation by minimizing Euclidean distance in UMAP space. Compute the ARI between these mapped synthetic labels and the original-data labels, alongside the Davies–Bouldin index of the synthetic partition. Repeat across all generative models (scDBM with Gibbs sampling, scVI prior, scVI posterior), the negative-binomial noise baseline, six pilot sizes (384, 768, 1152, 1536, 1920, 2304 cells), and 30 replicates per size, aggregating results with boxplot hinges at the 25th/75th percentiles. The rationale is that ARI measures similarity of clustering to ground truth while correcting for chance, so high ARI variability (as seen for scVI-posterior) signals amplification of small-dataset artifacts.

## Evaluation signals

- For each synthetic dataset, ARI is computed only after every synthetic cell has been assigned the label of its nearest original observation in UMAP Euclidean space — check no unlabeled cells remain
- Validate that the scVI-posterior variant exhibits the highest ARI/DBI variability across replicates at small pilot sizes (384–768 cells), sometimes performing worse than the noise baseline
- Confirm the reference DBI of the original Seurat clustering (orange dotted line in Figure 2) is included as a benchmark
- Check that results cover all six pilot sizes × 30 replicates for both PBMC4k and Segerstolpe before aggregation
- Verify Segerstolpe (Smart-seq2) ARI/DBI distributions are tighter and closer to the original-data DBI than the sparser PBMC4k (10x) ones

## Limitations

- The approach relies on the unverified assumption that synthetic cells mapped to the original data are also mapped to the correct clusters
- Evaluation of synthetic data quality is difficult, with sometimes ambiguous conclusions when examining multiple quality measures together
- For sparse 10x datasets, inference from small to larger datasets is more challenging, so ARI values from small pilots can be unstable; the scVI-posterior variant amplifies artifacts of small datasets
- All approaches overrepresented highly abundant cell types and missed less abundant ones, which biases cluster-similarity metrics toward abundant populations
