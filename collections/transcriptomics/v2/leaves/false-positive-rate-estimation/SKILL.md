# false-positive-rate-estimation

## Summary

Benchmark-style estimation of false-positive rates (and sensitivity/auROC) for Scanpro proportion analysis by simulating replicate datasets with sim_scanpro and scoring statistical calls (ANOVA/t-test) against a known simulated ground truth.

## When to use

Use when you need to quantify how many false-positive differential-proportion calls Scanpro produces — e.g., when choosing the number of bootstrapping pseudo-replicates (2–14) for a given total cell count per condition (~1,000 to ~50,000 cells).

## When NOT to use

- Your goal is the biological result itself (which cell types shift in proportion) rather than characterizing the statistical method's error behavior — run scanpro directly instead of a simulation sweep
- The dataset contains rare cell types supported by less than 2% of all cells, where benchmarking showed unstable results and limited bootstrapping benefit
- The input is already replicated per condition — with a real samples column, bootstrapping and pseudo-replicate tuning are unnecessary

## Inputs

- AnnData object (.h5ad) with clustered cell-type annotations and a condition column, used as the seed for simulated replicates
- Simulated replicate datasets generated by Scanpro's sim_scanpro function at defined total cell counts per condition
- Simulated ground-truth labels indicating which cell types have differential proportions

## Outputs

- Sensitivity and false-positive-rate estimates per pseudo-replicate count and transformation
- auROC as a function of mean total cell count per condition for each pseudo-replicate count (2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 10, 14)
- Overall accuracy values per pseudo-replicate count and variance-stabilizing transformation (logit, arcsin-sqrt)
- Benchmark panels (sensitivity/FPR, auROC-vs-mean-cell-count, accuracy) rendered via out.plot

## How to apply

Generate ~100 simulated replicate datasets per total-cell-count condition using Scanpro's sim_scanpro function, starting from real replicates (the paper used 4 original replicates) and sweeping 16 escalating total cell counts per condition from ~1,000 to ~50,000 cells. For each simulated dataset, run bootstrapping via run_scanpro with 2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 10, and 14 pseudo-replicates under both logit and arcsin-sqrt variance-stabilizing transformations. Call the underlying anova and t_test routines to obtain differential proportion calls, then record sensitivity and false-positive rates against the simulated ground truth. Compute auROC as a function of mean total cell count per condition plus overall accuracy for each pseudo-replicate count and transformation, and plot sensitivity/FPR, auROC-vs-cell-count, and accuracy panels with out.plot (boxplot kind where appropriate). The rationale is that bootstrapped pseudo-replicates only control error rates within certain cell-count regimes: 2 and 4 pseudo-replicates achieved the best auROC up to ~5,000 mean cells per condition, while 8–14 performed better above ~25,000 cells.

## Evaluation signals

- Reproduced auROC curves should show good auROC for 2 and 4 pseudo-replicates up to ~5,000 mean cells per condition and for 8–14 pseudo-replicates above ~25,000 cells
- False-positive rates must be computed against the simulated ground truth, not against real data lacking a known answer
- Both logit and arcsin-sqrt transformations should be swept; results reported for each, not just one
- The sweep should cover escalating total cell counts (~1,000 to ~50,000 cells) with ~100 simulated replicates per point, not a single dataset
- Clusters supported by less than 2% of all cells should be flagged as producing unstable results per the paper's benchmarking

## Limitations

- Bootstrapping has limited benefits when handling rare cell types, as indicated by the benchmarking test and real datasets
- Clusters supported by less than 2% of all cells produce unstable results, contaminating sensitivity/FPR estimates
- Bootstrapping-based results should be interpreted with caution and require additional experimental validation
- Bootstrapping often, but not always, yields the same conclusions as truly replicated datasets
