# deg-method-performance-evaluation

## Summary

Evaluates the classification performance of individual DEG detection methods (DESeq2, edgeR, limma, NOISeq) and integrated methods (combined-FDR, vote thresholds, Naïve Bayes classifier) by ranking genes and computing AUC against simulated ground-truth labels across varying logFC conditions.

## When to use

Use when simulated RNA-seq count matrices with known differentially expressed gene labels are available and you need to compare DEG detection methods by AUC, or to benchmark a combined/integrated method against individual tools.

## When NOT to use

- When only a real dataset without ground-truth DEG labels is available — AUC against simulated truth cannot be computed, and the article notes vote-threshold optima may not generalize across organisms, tissues and experimental designs
- When the goal is differential expression calling on a single real dataset rather than benchmarking method performance — use the DEgenes Hunter pipeline directly instead of the simulation-based evaluation

## Inputs

- Simulated RNA-seq count matrices with ground-truth differentially expressed gene labels (from upstream simulation, e.g. A. thaliana-derived, varying logFC conditions)
- Spike-in dataset count tables (FASTQ-derived, cleaned and mapped) with known truth sets
- Per-method p-values/significance measures from DESeq2, limma, NOISeq runs in DEgenes Hunter

## Outputs

- AUC per DEG method per simulated condition assembled into a results table (e.g. auc_comparison_fig2.csv)
- F-measure at each vote threshold for threshold-based combination methods
- HTML results report generated by the ExpHunterSuiteExamples launch/get_results scripts

## How to apply

Run each individual DEG detection method (DESeq2, limma, NOISeq) on the simulated count matrices through the DEgenes Hunter pipeline of the ExpHunter Suite, then compute integrated calls by p-value integration (combined-FDR) across the per-method results. For each method and integration approach, rank genes by their significance measure and compute the AUC against the simulated ground-truth DEG labels for every simulated logFC condition. Assemble AUC values per method per condition into a single results table (e.g. auc_comparison_fig2.csv) and check that the relative method ordering matches the published trends: no single method wins in all scenarios, but combined-FDR tends to perform well. The article also benchmarks vote-threshold combinations (optimum threshold 2 by F-measure on spike-in data) and a Naïve Bayes classifier trained on simulated data, which matched the best single and combination methods on the spike-in dataset. Methods can behave quite differently depending on data properties, so performance must be assessed per condition rather than averaged unconditionally.

Rscript -e 'devtools::install_github("seoanezonjic/ExpHunterSuite", dependencies=TRUE)'

## Evaluation signals

- AUC table (auc_comparison_fig2.csv) is populated for every method and every simulated logFC condition
- Relative method ordering reproduces the Fig. 2 trend: no single method best in all scenarios, combined-FDR generally strong
- Spike-in benchmark: vote threshold 2 maximizes F-measure, and Naïve Bayes classifier performs as well as the best single and combination methods
- Individual method signatures are consistent with the article: DESeq2 most conservative on Lafora data, limma higher precision, NOISeq high recall

## Limitations

- No single DEG detection method performs best in all simulated scenarios, so conclusions are condition-specific
- The optimum vote threshold (2 for spike-in data) may differ for other organisms, tissues and experimental designs; the authors are hesitant to extrapolate it as a general rule
- A competing recommendation of much higher vote thresholds was based on the analysis of a single human dataset, limiting comparability
- More elaborate classifiers than Naïve Bayes were left for future work; stable implementations of the proposed methodologies are still needed
